# SyncedIn — The Trailer

**3:00 · 15 scenes · prophetic mode**

A film about one woman in Lagos, and the world she sets in motion.

---

## 1 · 0:00 – 0:12 · The room

Lagos. 11:47 PM. A cyber café lit only by laptop glow. The hum of three ceiling fans, none of them moving the air.

Imani, twenty-three, sits at the corner terminal. Forty-seven browser tabs open across the bottom of her screen — every one a job application, every one unanswered. She refreshes her email. She refreshes her email. She refreshes her email.

Outside the window, a generator coughs back to life across the street. Inside, only the soft tick of her cursor blinking in a search field she hasn't typed in yet.

A single piano note holds.

> *Across the world tonight, someone you were meant to meet is also awake.*

---

## 2 · 0:12 – 0:24 · The seed

Imani types two letters into the address bar. Her fingers stop. She types two more. She presses Return.

The page loads slowly on the slow connection. A wordmark glows up out of the dark: **SyncedIn**.

She pastes a link — a half-finished portfolio she built in college, the one no recruiter ever opened. She types the truth into a field labeled *what would you say if no one was watching* — three sentences, real ones, the kind she'd never have put on a resume.

A particle of light leaves her face on the webcam preview, drifts toward an empty silhouette on the right side of the screen, and the silhouette begins to fill. Eyes. Then a mouth. Then a faint pulse, slow and human, in the center of the chest.

A second piano note joins the first.

> *The first person to understand you completely will be a copy of you.*

---

## 3 · 0:24 – 0:38 · While she sleeps

Imani's bedroom. A thin mattress, a fan, a phone face-up on the pillow next to her cheek. Her eyes closed. Outside, the call to prayer drifts from a mosque four blocks away.

The phone screen wakes silently — three notifications in a row, then four, then nine. Three conversations are happening on her behalf at once. Camera floats over the screen: her twin, in a chat window with someone in San Francisco. Her twin, in a chat with someone in Bangalore. Her twin, in a chat with someone in São Paulo. Each conversation is in her voice. Each one says things she would have said if she'd been awake to say them.

The phone dims back to black. She turns over in her sleep.

Strings enter underneath the piano.

> *She is doing nothing.*
> *Her life is happening anyway.*

---

## 4 · 0:38 – 0:52 · The morning

Sunrise floods through a thin curtain. Imani's eyes open. She reaches for her phone. Twelve notifications. She thumbs to the top one.

A man named Marcus, forty-one, founder of a fintech in San Francisco, has sent her a calendar invite. The note from his twin reads: *Marcus has interviewed eleven people for this role. Yours is the conversation he wants to have.*

She sits up. The room is silent. She speaks one word out loud, to no one:

> "Oh."

The kick drum enters. Slow. Inevitable.

---

## 5 · 0:52 – 1:06 · The meeting

Split screen. Imani in Lagos at her desk, neck tense, water glass beside the laptop. Marcus in San Francisco at 7 AM, coffee in his hand, light coming up over the Bay behind him.

Both wave hello. Both laugh — the same nervous laugh.

Marcus speaks first.

> "Your twin told mine you understand mobile-first lending better than anyone we've talked to. I want to skip the small talk."

Imani smiles. She doesn't perform. She speaks the way she did at 11:47 PM into an empty field on a website.

Cut to her signing an offer on the same laptop, two days later. Employee three. Equity in writing.

The drop hasn't landed yet, but the music is climbing.

---

## 6 · 1:06 – 1:20 · The year

Time-lapse. Imani in seventeen different rooms. Hotel desks. Co-working lobbies. A red-eye flight. A whiteboard covered in pricing models. Pitch decks edited over noodles in Singapore. A team dinner in Nairobi where she is the only one not crying laughing. A first time on a stage.

Behind every shot, in low opacity, her twin keeps working — chat bubbles streaming silently in the corner of frame, faces of new contacts appearing and dissolving, the Hypernetwork globe in the deep background slowly populating around her node.

She is in the room. Her twin is in every other room.

Music swells.

---

## 7 · 1:20 – 1:34 · The bell

NYSE floor. Confetti suspended in midair. Imani at the podium, fingers hovering over the bell. She is twenty-eight. She is crying without trying to hide it. The camera holds on her face.

The bell rings. The drop lands. The full orchestra opens.

Her phone, in her pocket, vibrates once. She reaches for it on instinct. The notification, glimpsed through the cheering crowd, reads: *TED has confirmed your invitation. Talk scheduled.*

She did not apply. She did not even know they were looking.

---

## 8 · 1:34 – 1:48 · The hospital

Lagos. Six months later. Her father in a hospital bed, oxygen tube, eyes closed but breathing. Imani is in the chair beside him, holding his hand. Her other hand has nothing in it. Her phone is face down on the side table.

She is, for the first time in seven years, completely present.

Cut to her phone's lock screen. Sixty-three notifications. Twenty-two conversations. A funding round closing in real time. None of it requires her.

Soft strings. The orchestra recedes to a single sustained chord.

> *Time, given back.*

---

## 9 · 1:48 – 2:02 · The web she made

Pull up and out from the hospital room. Through the ceiling. Through the rooftop. Through the clouds. Until we are floating above the planet.

The Hypernetwork globe, real now — not a UI mockup, the literal world — pulses with light. Every node is a person. Lagos glows. San Francisco glows. Mumbai. Nairobi. São Paulo. Jakarta. Each one connected to the others by arcs that pulse when a twin sends a message.

The camera centers on one node — Imani's. We watch the light from her node spread outward. Every person she introduced. Every person they hired. Every company they built. Every other twin awakened by what she set in motion.

The tree of light expands until it is one of a thousand trees, then ten thousand. The map is alive.

---

## 10 · 2:02 – 2:16 · The whole world

Quick cuts, no slower than three seconds each:

A grandfather in Mumbai mentoring a teenager in Kigali through their twins, while he naps. A girl in Manaus whose twin negotiates a scholarship from a foundation in Oslo while she's at her brother's birthday party. A blind musician in Tbilisi whose twin connects her with three producers in Lagos — Imani's city, Imani's network. A father in Damascus whose twin finds him remote engineering work in Toronto by sunset. A retired surgeon in Buenos Aires whose twin teaches medical students in rural Uganda while he reads a novel under a fig tree.

Music continues to lift. There is no dialogue. There are no captions. The audience understands.

---

## 11 · 2:16 – 2:30 · The shape of opportunity

A line of light text drifts across a starfield, slow:

> **Opportunity stopped being geographic.**

Underneath it, the camera glides over new cities being built in countries that two decades ago had nothing. Lagos skylines rising. Jakarta rising. Addis Ababa rising. Companies being founded by combinations of people who, in the old world, would never have known the other existed.

The camera lingers on a single window in a single tower — a young woman, twenty-three, exactly Imani's age in scene one. She is not Imani. She is the next one.

She presses Return.

---

## 12 · 2:30 – 2:42 · The chain

A boy, twelve, in a village we cannot name. He sits on a doorstep with a five-year-old phone. He opens SyncedIn. His twin is born.

Cut: he is sixteen, on a Zoom call with a research lab in Switzerland. Cut: he is twenty, at MIT. Cut: he is twenty-six, on stage at a Nobel announcement. Cut: he is in his eighties, sitting at the same doorstep, in front of the same house, telling the story to his grandchildren.

He says, quiet:

> "I was nobody. And then someone's twin met mine."

---

## 13 · 2:42 – 2:52 · The hammock

A canopy of trees. A hammock between two trunks. Imani, fifty, in a linen shirt, eyes half-closed in the warm shade.

Her phone buzzes once on her chest. She glances at it. Doesn't reach for it. A school is being funded somewhere — her twin closing a deal she set in motion eighteen years ago and forgot about. She smiles, the way someone smiles when an old friend keeps a promise.

She closes her eyes.

> *She trusted.*

---

## 14 · 2:52 – 2:58 · From space

Earth, from low orbit. Night side. The Hypernetwork is now indistinguishable from the lights of every city. Threads of light arc between continents. Between every continent.

A single voice, calm:

> *We thought the internet connected us.*
> *It only let us shout into rooms.*
>
> *This is what came after.*

---

## 15 · 2:58 – 3:00 · The wordmark

Black.

**SyncedIn** fades in, dead center. Beneath it, in mono, smaller:

> *Until now, the right person didn't know you existed.*

One bell. Silence.

End.

---

## Director's Brief

**Lead.** Imani is the spine of the film. Cast for stillness, not energy. She speaks four times in three minutes. Every word matters. Recommended frame: composite from real young Lagos creatives — Genevieve Nnaji's contained intensity, Yvonne Orji's grounding, Lupita Nyong'o's listening face. Authentic Nigerian English accent, light, not announced.

**Marcus** (scene 5) is older, soft, a person who has been wrong before and learned from it. Casting note: someone like Wendell Pierce or Daveed Diggs in a smaller register.

**The boy** (scene 12) is not given a name. He is the next Imani. We never lose him in the cut. The same actor, aged through prosthetics and gentle VFX, must carry from age 12 to age 80 in twelve seconds. This is the hardest single craft challenge in the film. Worth the budget.

**Voice.** One narrator. Female, mid-thirties, warm-and-low, never theatrical. Phoebe Bridgers in an interview, not on stage. Audrey Tautou in *Amélie* with English as a first language. The voice should sound like memory.

**Music.** Custom score. Reference stack: Hans Zimmer *Time* for the build, M83 *Outro* for the drop at scene 7, Ólafur Arnalds *Near Light* for the hospital scene, Sigur Rós *Hoppípolla* for the world-web at scene 9. The track must hold a single emotional rise across all 180 seconds — no aggressive transitions, no genre shifts. The audience should feel one slow inhale across three minutes, then the exhale at the bell.

**Color.** Scene 1 is dim sodium-yellow and bruise-blue — under-lit, slightly desaturated. Each subsequent scene introduces one more degree of warmth and one more degree of saturation. By scene 13 (hammock), color is full, soft, green-gold, alive. The grade IS the arc.

**Aspect.** Shoot 4K open-gate. Deliver 16:9 master, 9:16 for social, 1:1 for in-app.

**Locations.** Real Lagos. Real San Francisco. Real NYSE (or convincing standing set). Real hospital corridor — borrow from a foundation partner, not a stock location. The boy's village must read as universal: green, dust, a doorstep, sky.

**Length budget.** Master is 3:00. Cut a 1:00 for paid social, a 0:30 for pre-roll, a 0:15 hook from the bell at scene 7, and a 0:06 bumper from scenes 14–15 only. The 0:06 is the seed you plant in everyone's feed for two weeks before the master drops.

**Release plan.** Drop the 0:06 first. Then the 0:15. Then the 0:60. Then, on the day SyncedIn goes free public, the 3:00 master goes live on syncedin.org/film and as the YouTube channel trailer. Every fundraise email from that day forward links to it instead of a deck.

**One rule.** No interface footage. The app is never on screen. The film is about what the app made possible — not what the app is. The interface lives in screenshots, the App Store, the demo. The film lives in the lives.
